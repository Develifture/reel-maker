# Script templates

Seven structures taken from high-view reels. Word counts assume about 3.4 spoken words per second (seconds = words / 3.4). Beat names are order only: the source data had unreliable timestamps, so plan by order and word budget, not clock times.

| Length | Spoken words |
|---|---|
| 15 to 30s | about 85 |
| 30 to 45s | about 135 |
| 45 to 60s | about 175 |
| 60 to 90s | about 230 |

Most common structure (68 of 199 reels): **hook, setup, value, proof, call-to-action.** Next: hook, value, proof, call-to-action (26); hook, value, call-to-action (17).

## T1. Tool tutorial (35 to 50s, 130 to 175 words)
Based on a Claude Code plus Google Stitch reel (@nick_saraev, 1.19M, 45s).
| Beat | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 12 | State the gap: A is great, B is the weak spot. |
| Setup | 20 | Name the free fix and say what it does in one sentence. |
| Value | 80 to 100 | 3 to 4 steps. Each is "go here, do this, get that". Screen recording throughout. |
| Proof | 20 | The result appearing: "and just like that, it now has..." |
| Call-to-action | 25 | Keyword plus what it unlocks. |
Visual plan: face for hook and setup, then screen recording with zoom-ins and a text label per step, then the result full screen.

## T2. Curated list (40 to 45s, 140 to 170 words)
Based on a "five skills" reel (@nick_saraev, 906K, 44s).
| Beat | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 14 | Scarcity: huge number, you only need few. |
| Items | 25 each | "First is [name]. You just [action] and it [benefit]." |
| Call-to-action | 25 | "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send them all." |
Rules: each item is name, what it does, benefit. Put the item name on screen as it is said. Odd numbers (3, 5) read cleanly.

## T3. Instant tip (20 to 25s, 70 to 90 words)
Based on a no-face VS Code tip (@acadeller, 1.83M, 23s).
- Hook: a "did you know" sentence that already contains the action and the result.
- Show the steps in real time on screen, no cuts away from the screen.
- Close on the finished result, one sentence, then a plain ask ("like for more like this").
Use when the topic is one trick and a keyword funnel would add friction.

## T4. Price-anchor replacement (18 to 35s, 60 to 110 words)
Based on @marc.kaz (245K to 894K).
- Casual spoken opener, then the claim: free or open-source alternative to a paid tool.
- Pain in one line: what the paid version costs or limits.
- 3 to 5 one-line facts (license, self-hosted, features, one command to run).
- Proof: the repo page or product on screen, with the link or name as text.
- Link in caption or bio; keyword optional.

## T5. Stakes list (60 to 70s, about 230 words)
Based on "your app can get sued" (@adilet.fndr, 877K, 70s).
| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| Hook | Dollar stake, one sentence, with a promise of a fix at the end. |
| Proof x N | N risks, each with a concrete number and source in one or two sentences. One visual card per risk. |
| Twist | One line that reframes the pattern ("the word is per"). |
| Fix | One prompt or action, then a share or follow ask ("send this video to Claude"). |
Caution: every number must be real and sourced.

## T6. Problem demo, then named fix (55 to 70s)
Based on the "LLM council" reel (@kimball.ai, 1.82M, 69s).
- Hook: the failure everyone knows ("it agrees with everything").
- Setup: show it live with a real prompt, then flip the question to expose the flaw.
- Value: the fix, named, in three parts.
- Proof: one real example of the improved answer.
- Call-to-action: keyword for the prompt or file.

## T7. Skit or dialogue (50 to 65s)
Based on Family Guy characters over game footage (@algorithmswithpeter, 771K, 58s) and a staged terminal skit (@programmergrind, 1.1M, 64s).
- Two voices: one asks what the viewer is thinking, one answers with the facts.
- One idea per exchange, two sentences at most per line.
- Big word-by-word captions in the lower third. Slides or cards in the top half, one per sentence.
- End with a character-delivered call-to-action, in character.
Trade-off: high likes per view, low comments unless you add a keyword offer.

## Universal rules
- Write the call-to-action first, then write backwards: it decides what you promise in the hook.
- Cut any sentence that does not give a fact, a step or proof.
- Read aloud with a timer. If it runs 15% over the target, cut words rather than speed up.
- Plan a visual change at least every 3 to 4 seconds.
